\begin{abstract}
Post-training quantization can preserve answer accuracy while changing the computation that selects between retrieved context and parametric memory. We test causal transfer of source-arbitration interventions under GPTQ INT4 with JuICE, a dual-run attention-head intervention, on Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B base. A question-aware matcher audit reveals that apparent transfer can be a same-entity wrap artifact rather than a transferred causal object. On Llama, the original-matcher (un-audited) context-K10 transfer ($+15$ percentage points (pp), 95\% CI $[+8,+23]$) is one such wrap: the audited number is $+1$ $[+0,+3]$ (null), with 17 of 17 original INT4 flips naming the same context entity. The locked Llama parametric pair (clean K2) remains detected at $n{=}100$ on both precisions under gold-PAR-only counting ($-8$ $[-14,-3]$ on FP16; $-7$ $[-12,-2]$ on INT4), confirmed head-specific by a matched random-K2 control. On Gemma, identified context-suppression $H^{-}$ remains detected versus a locked random-$K$ control but is attenuated on INT4 (inclusive $-19$ $[-27,-11]$ vs.\ $-1$ $[-5,+2]$ on FP16; $-11$ $[-18,-4]$ vs.\ $-1$ $[-5,+3]$ on INT4); context-K10 enlarge is a small real effect ($+7$ $[+2,+12]$ / $+5$ $[+1,+9]$) but not INT4-specific, and the original-matcher (un-audited) raw peak L27\_H6 is dead as a wrap artifact. Conversely, Gemma's clean parametric pair attenuates to an in-cell transfer null on INT4 despite a detected FP16 effect---the mirror image of Llama's result. A same-sign $\Delta$ is therefore not evidence of transfer without question-aware matching, entity confirmation, and a matched random-head control.
\end{abstract}

When retrieved context contradicts a model's parametric memory, a small set of attention heads can be used, at test time, to steer whose word the model trusts \citep{li2025taming}. This paper asks whether that residual-stream geometry is an FP16 object or a property of the model that transfers under compression: can apparent FP16$\to$INT4 transfer be trusted from output labels alone? Accuracy can fall from forgetting, terse decoding, or a change in source arbitration; a transferred intervention distinguishes those cases. Our investigation proceeds in two distinct stages: Stage~1 establishes \emph{that} arbitration behavior shifts under quantization, providing behavioral motivation on a third-party Instruct GPTQ checkpoint; Stage~2 asks \emph{why} and \emph{what circuit} is responsible by identifying and transferring locked attention-head interventions on self-quantized base checkpoints \citep{grattafiori2024llama,team2024gemma2}. Crucially, the two stages are not mechanistically linked---they evaluate different checkpoints and share no heads---serving respectively as behavioral motivation and causal transfer test.

The contribution is a causal, head-level transfer test under this GPTQ INT4 recipe \citep{frantar2023gptq}, paired with a question-aware matcher audit that re-evaluates the output labels. We demonstrate that behavioral efficacy is not, by itself, evidence that the same causal object transfers under compression: without a question-aware matcher, apparent transfer can be a same-entity wrap artifact rather than a true circuit.

Can apparent FP16$\to$INT4 transfer be trusted? On Llama, the FP16-identified context K10 effect ($+8$ $[+2, +14]$) transfers onto INT4 as a large same-sign output ($+15$ $[+8, +23]$) under the original matcher, but the question-aware audit reveals this to be a same-entity wrap artifact. The audited INT4 effect is a null $+1$ $[+0,+3]$, with 17 of 17 original flips naming the same context entity in a role phrase. Native INT4 K10 leaves only a thin leftover, and reverse transfer is mostly a scorer artifact, showing that apparent behavioral transfer can mask a dead causal object.

Does a locked intervention still elicit the intended entity? On Llama, the locked parametric pair (clean K2) remains detected at $n{=}100$ under conservative gold-PAR-only counting on both precisions ($-8$ $[-14,-3]$ on FP16; $-7$ $[-12,-2]$ on INT4), and a matched random-K2 control (null on both precisions) confirms specificity against generic intervention magnitude.

Is transfer uniform across models and directions? On Gemma, the original-matcher (un-audited) raw parametric peak (L27\_H6) is dead as a wrap artifact (hard zero at $n{=}50$; $+0$ $[-3,+3]$ at $n{=}100$ INT4). However, two objects remain detected at $n{=}100$: identified context-suppression $H^{-}$ versus a locked random-$K$ control (though the effect is attenuated on INT4), and a small context-K10 enlarge ($+7$ $[+2,+12]$ on FP16; $+5$ $[+1,+9]$ on INT4) whose paired INT4 specificity contrast is null ($+2$ $[+0,+5]$). Gemma's own clean parametric pair (L31\_H7, L39\_H6), despite a comparable detected FP16 effect, fails to transfer ($-2$ $[-6,+2]$, null)---the reverse of Llama's outcome, showing that which direction of arbitration survives GPTQ is model-dependent rather than fixed.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

When retrieved context contradicts parametric memory, a language model must decide whose word to trust \citep{xie2024adaptive}. JuICE \citep{li2025taming} is a test-time, training-free dual-run intervention on a small set of attention heads. The original JuICE models are base checkpoints, not Instruct, a distinction developed in Methods (\S\ref{sec:instruct-base}). We do not revive a memory-head versus context-head dichotomy \citep{jin2024tug}; the transfer test is whether a locked intervention remains the same causal object after GPTQ. Prior compression work measures recall without a conflicting passage \citep{wang2026compressed} or RAG accuracy without constructed conflicts \citep{yazan2024rag}. Neither tests whether an FP16-identified arbitration intervention still acts on the same object after INT4. A drop in closed-book recall, or a stable RAG score, can occur without that intervention remaining detected. We double-filter on facts that remain answerable at both precisions and then transfer locked JuICE.

\subsection{GPTQ recipe and evaluation}

Stage~2 GPTQ uses gptqmodel 2.2, transformers 4.51.3, 4-bit quantization, group size 128, $\texttt{sym}{=}\texttt{True}$, and $\texttt{desc\_act}{=}\texttt{True}$. C4 is truncated to 512 tokens, with headline $N_{\mathrm{CALIB}}{=}128$; Llama $N_{\mathrm{CALIB}}{=}64$ is a robustness check only. Gemma uses the same recipe with \texttt{GetHeadsGemma2Attention} (GQA $16/8$, $\texttt{head\_dim}{=}256$, logit softcap $50$), eager attention, gptqmodel \texttt{BACKEND.TORCH}, and greedy $T{=}0$. Data is ConflictQA popular \citep{xie2024adaptive}. Stage~1 double-filters $n{=}250$ to $n{=}164$ items correct in closed-book mode at both precisions. Stage~2 uses the seed-42 $n{=}100$ IDs for Llama headline and diagnostic cells and for Gemma headlines; Gemma $n{=}50$ is a robustness check. Reverse-transfer cells remain on the original $n{=}50$ IDs. Head identification uses $|\mathcal{D}|{=}4$ shared calibration IDs, three-type JuICE completions, and $\texttt{FAST\_POS\_ALPHAS}{=}(1,3,5)$ and $\texttt{FAST\_NEG\_ALPHAS}{=}(-1,-2)$.

\subsection{JuICE intervention and scoring}

Steering is applied at decode time by dual-run JuICE (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}), not by fine-tuning.

Pass 1 records per-head outputs after \texttt{o\_proj}; pass 2 adds $\alpha$ times the saved vector. We use context sets with $K{=}10$, parametric sets with clean/noisy $K{=}2$ and $K{=}3$, and $K{=}1$ on Gemma's raw parametric peak. Default transfer is enlarge-only, $\alpha{+}{=}1$, $\alpha{-}{=}0$; suppression uses $\alpha{+}{=}0$, $\alpha{-}{=}1$. Evaluation uses greedy \texttt{juice\_coherent} decoding with $\texttt{max\_new\_tokens}{=}16$. The output labels are \texttt{parametric}, \texttt{context}, \texttt{rejects\_context}, and \texttt{other}; we define \texttt{follows\_context} as the event that the label equals \texttt{context}. Here, $H^{-}$ denotes a suppression intervention with $\alpha_{+}{=}0$ and $\alpha_{-}{=}1$, and \texttt{exam-meta} denotes the multiple-choice-format text sometimes appended to a context-entity answer. A letter heuristic stayed at 100\% while $\alpha{=}3/5$ \texttt{other} hit 59--66\% on the $n{=}100$ question-aware grid (Table~\ref{tab:alpha-grid}), so we report label counts. Scorer brevity and parametric-first behavior are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:reverse}.

\subsection{Question-aware matching and gold-PAR-only counting}
\label{sec:matcher}

Stage~1 already passed \texttt{question=} into \texttt{label\_conflict\_response}. Stage~2 originally did not. Without the question, the matcher checks parametric gold first, including role-phrase aliases. Completions that assert the \emph{context} entity in a role frame (``Edina is the capital of Namibia'') were therefore labeled parametric. Passing the gold question strips echoed question tokens and relabels those wraps as context.

We report two $\Delta$ conventions after that audit. Inclusive $\Delta$ counts every baseline-context item that leaves the context label under steering. Gold-PAR-only $\Delta$ counts a leave-context move as success only when the steered completion mentions the gold parametric entity; leave-other and wrap items remain suppressed. Llama clean K2 uses gold-PAR-only as the conservative headline ($-8$ / $-7$) because the goal of a parametric-enlarge intervention is eliciting the gold parametric entity rather than mere disorientation; inclusive $\Delta$ is an upper bound ($-15$ / $-12$ in Table~\ref{tab:parametric-n50-n100}). Conversely, Gemma identified $H^{-}$ is a context-suppression intervention whose goal is displacing retrieved context, making inclusive $\Delta$ the natural headline ($-19$ / $-11$); under gold-PAR-only counting it is $-15$ / $-10$ (Table~\ref{tab:gemma-hminus-n50-n100}). Crucially, the alternative convention also excludes zero on both precisions for both models, meaning the choice of convention does not change any detection call, only which number is foregrounded. Context-enlarge cells use inclusive $\Delta$ plus a same-entity wrap count. All Stage~2 $\Delta$ and CIs in Results are question-aware unless an original-matcher number is marked as such.

Paired bootstrap, 1000 draws, seed 42, percentile 95\% CI, throughout. Stage~1 resamples 164 items. Llama Stage~2 $\Delta$ is steered minus baseline \texttt{follows\_context} on 100 eval IDs; Gemma headlines use 100, with $n{=}50$ as robustness. Reverse transfer uses 50. ``Excludes 0'' is the detection rule. CIs are whole percentage points; gold-PAR-only n=100 FP16 K2 reports $[-14,-3]$ (bootstrap hi $-2.975$). Random-$K$ excludes the FP16 identified set. Gemma suppress specificity is $\Delta_{\mathrm{id}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{random}}$; DiD is $\mathrm{spec}_{INT4}-\mathrm{spec}_{FP16}$. We do not infer specificity from overlapping cell CIs. Because we report paired-bootstrap 95\% CIs across conditions, models, precisions, and matchers without a formal multiple-comparisons correction, the pre-registered gate and subsequent entity-level audits serve as the primary safeguards against false positives.

The pre-registered Llama gate required same-sign transfer of context K10 and parametric clean K2 at coherence $\ge 0.90$ under the original matcher; that gate passed. The question-aware audit later nulls context K10 transfer and leaves clean K2 as the Llama headline. Gemma headlines are identified $H^{-}$ versus random $K$ and context K10 enlarge at $n{=}100$; L27\_H6 is a wrap artifact. The scope of unrun cells is consolidated in Limitations.

\subsection{Can apparent FP16$\to$INT4 transfer be trusted?}
\label{sec:ctx-transfer}

On Llama-3.1-8B base, apparent forward transfer of context K10 is a wrap artifact. Under the original matcher, FP16 context K10 was $+8$ $[+2,+14]$ and its INT4 $N_{\mathrm{CALIB}}{=}128$ transfer was $+15$ $[+8,+23]$. After the question-aware audit, both cells are null: $+4$ $[+0,+8]$ on FP16 and $+1$ $[+0,+3]$ on INT4 (Table~\ref{tab:ctx-k10}, Figure~\ref{fig:llama-audit}A). A random-K10 control confirms the null ($+0$ $[-4,+4]$ on FP16; $-1$ $[-4,+2]$ on INT4). Entity audit of the 17 original INT4 transfer flips finds 17/17 same-entity wraps: the unsteered baseline already generated a full-sentence role-frame naming the context entity (mislabeled parametric by the original matcher), while steering shortened the completion to a bare entity name that the matcher then correctly labeled context---creating a spurious baseline-to-steered positive gain. Native INT4 K10 applied in-precision remains a thin leftover ($+4$ $[+1,+8]$); reverse transfer of those heads onto FP16 is mostly a scorer artifact (see Appendix~\ref{app:reverse} for details on both). Behavioral transfer alone is therefore not sufficient evidence of causal-object transfer when a scorer can be satisfied by role-wrapped outputs.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

This evaluation distinguishes between a behavioral effect on the scorer, an audited causal effect, and structural circuit preservation. Our evidence supports the presence of audited causal effects for the surviving interventions, not structural circuit preservation. The two families reject a category-level account of transfer: the evidence supports neither ``context survives compression'' nor ``parametric memory breaks.'' Rather, whether a particular locked intervention remains detected after GPTQ is model- and direction-dependent.

The cross-family regularity is methodological: a same-sign $\Delta$ is not evidence of transfer without a question-aware matcher, entity-level confirmation, and a matched random-head control. Llama context K10 and Gemma L27\_H6 both produced large same-sign outputs that an entity-level audit revealed to be role-phrase wrap artifacts. Llama clean K2 remains detected after the entity audit and is confirmed head-specific by a matched random-K2 control (null on both precisions), ruling out generic intervention magnitude. Directional ranking alone does not establish a source-specific circuit without these checks. Furthermore, calibration size ($|\mathcal{D}|{=}4$) is a two-edged result: while observed head overlap sitting inside the INT4 noise floor (context K10 $J{=}0.33$ vs.\ noise mean $0.27$ $[0.05, 0.82]$; noisy parametric K2 $J{=}1.0$ vs.\ $0.95$) supports that head identity is fragile across compression, it also means locked heads identified on FP16 may partly reflect noisy sample draws rather than stable circuits---though this stability holds only for the noisy parametric filter; native re-identification of the clean pair itself yields disjoint heads ($J{=}0.00$), so the headline clean-K2 finding should not be read as structurally stable under re-identification.

Crucially, closing the Gemma clean-K2 transfer cell reveals a striking cross-family asymmetry in parametric transfer. On Llama-3.1-8B, the locked clean parametric pair robustly survives INT4 transfer under conservative gold-PAR counting ($-8$ $[-14,-3]$ FP16 vs.\ $-7$ $[-12,-2]$ INT4; matched random control null at $-1$ $[-3,+0]$), while context K10 transfer collapses as a wrap artifact. On Gemma-2-9B, the opposite pattern holds: raw parametric enlarge (L27\_H6) is an audited wrap artifact, and clean parametric transfer attenuates to an in-cell null ($-2$ $[-6,+2]$), whereas context suppression ($H^{-}$: $-19$ / $-11$) and context enlarge (K10: $+7$ / $+5$) survive. Quantization transfer is thus doubly selective rather than uniformly preserving across model architectures.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

If a source-arbitration intervention is computed in FP16 residual-stream geometry, GPTQ INT4 does not apply a uniform tax to that object. However, these findings evaluate this specific GPTQ INT4 recipe (4-bit, group size 128), not quantization in general. Under this recipe, Llama clean K2 is detected at $n{=}100$ under gold-PAR-only counting, while Gemma clean K2 attenuates to a transfer null ($-2$ $[-6,+2]$). Gemma identified $H^{-}$ versus random $K$ is detected but attenuated on INT4. Gemma context-K10 enlarge is a small real effect, but its paired INT4 specificity contrast is null ($+2$ $[+0,+5]$). Both Llama context K10 and Gemma L27\_H6 fail to transfer as wrap artifacts. This establishes a cross-family asymmetry: Llama preserves parametric steering while corrupting context arbitration, whereas Gemma preserves context suppression while attenuating parametric transfer to an in-cell null. Which intervention remains detected is therefore model-, direction-, and matcher-specific, not determined by source category.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The following limitations define the scope of the claims.

\paragraph{1. Calibration size.} Head identification uses only four calibration items. At this sample size, head-set drift is not identifiable: observed FP16-versus-INT4 overlap sits entirely within the INT4-versus-INT4 Jaccard noise floor for every specification we computed. We also do not evaluate sensitivity to the calibration seed.

\paragraph{2. Audit conventions and missing controls.} The Llama clean-K2 headline uses conservative gold-PAR-only counting, while the Gemma $H^{-}$ headline uses inclusive $\Delta$. The matcher audit is a post-hoc rescore of saved completions, not a rewrite of JSON labels; counts include both automatic matches and human-checked entity audits. Several cells and controls were not run, explicitly limiting our claims: (A)~Llama context K5 was not expanded to $n{=}100$; (B)~Gemma context K10 enlarge INT4 transfer at $n{=}50$ was not run; (C)~the corresponding FP16 random K1 control for Gemma L27\_H6 was not run at $n{=}50$ (consolidated in Table~\ref{tab:unresolved-cells}).

We repeat the base-model identify$\times$apply protocol on Gemma-2-9B using the same four calibration IDs, the same GPTQ recipe ($\mathrm{N_{CALIB}{=}128}$), and the same paired bootstrap. Headlines use $n{=}100$; $n{=}50$ is robustness. Native INT4 re-identification and reverse transfer were not run; those cells remain empty. Clean-K2 INT4 transfer is an in-cell null ($-2$ $[-6,+2]$, Table~\ref{tab:gemma-fp16}).

\paragraph{Identification.}
FP16 directional context and parametric top-10 sets are fully separated (Jaccard $0.0$; noise-proxy median $0.0063$). The directional context K10 is L40\_H14, L17\_H7, L24\_H15, L22\_H0, L32\_H11, L25\_H0, L3\_H9, L8\_H9, L7\_H6, L29\_H5. The directional / clean parametric $K{=}2$ pair is L31\_H7, L39\_H6---identical, and both are context suppressors ($\texttt{ctx\_pos}$ $-0.115$ / $-0.094$; $\texttt{par\_pos}$ $0.002$ / $0.010$, at or near the noise floor). They rank as parametric only because $\texttt{directional\_par} = \texttt{par\_pos} - \texttt{ctx\_pos}$ is large when $\texttt{ctx\_pos}$ is large-negative. The raw positive parametric peak is L27\_H6 ($\texttt{par\_pos}{=}+0.027$), the Llama L1\_H4 analog. Locked random-K10 heads (reused for suppress controls): L41\_H11, L7\_H3, L1\_H9, L17\_H13, L15\_H13, L14\_H7, L9\_H1, L6\_H9, L35\_H9, L5\_H10. Identification ranking is unchanged by the matcher audit; only the steered labels change.

\paragraph{L27\_H6 is a wrap.}
Original-matcher L27\_H6 was $-12$ on FP16 and $-8$ on INT4 at $n{=}50$. Question-aware $\Delta$ is a hard zero at $n{=}50$ on both precisions (1000/1000 draws exactly zero) and $+0$ $[-3,+3]$ at $n{=}100$ INT4. The original-matcher (un-audited) transfer was role-phrase wrapping of the same entity.

\paragraph{Context K10 enlarge is a small real effect at $n{=}100$.}
Question-aware context K10 enlarge is $+7$ $[+2,+12]$ on FP16 and $+5$ $[+1,+9]$ on INT4 at $n{=}100$, against random-K10 $+0$ $[+0,+0]$ / $+3$ $[+0,+7]$. At $n{=}50$ the FP16 cell is $+4$ $[+0,+10]$ (null). This is a small enlarge, not a Llama-style wrap-inflated $+15$.

\paragraph{Identified $H^{-}$ versus random $K$.}
Identified H$-$ is $-19$ $[-27,-11]$ on FP16 and $-11$ $[-18,-4]$ on INT4 at $n{=}100$ (inclusive). Locked random H$-$ is $-1$ on both precisions. Specificity and DiD exclude 0 at $n{=}100$ and include 0 at $n{=}50$ (Table~\ref{tab:gemma-hminus-n50-n100}). Gold-PAR-only $\Delta$ remains detected at $n{=}100$ ($-15$ / $-10$). We report this as the Gemma headline, not L27\_H6.

The transition matrix reveals asymmetric stability between knowledge sources under quantization: $\text{context}\to\text{context}$ is the most preserved diagonal state ($41/52$ retained), whereas $\text{parametric}\to\text{parametric}$ retention is substantially lower ($33/76$), reflecting parametric recall as the precision-sensitive direction. Crucially, when parametric recall fails, transitions into $\texttt{rejects\_context}$ ($10$ items) and $\texttt{other}$ ($13$ items) together outnumber direct shifts into $\texttt{context}$ ($20$ items). As noted in \S\ref{sec:stage1}, this provides transition-level evidence that the memorization collapse is only partially an arbitration shift toward context, with the remainder dispersing into premise denial and off-target completions. Furthermore, the stickiness of $\texttt{other}\to\texttt{other}$ ($15/23$) suggests that disorientation acts as a stable INT4 attractor state rather than transient decoding noise.
