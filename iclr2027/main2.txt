Post-training quantization can preserve answer accuracy while changing the computation that selects between retrieved context and parametric memory. We test
causal transfer of source-arbitration interventions under GPTQ INT4 with JuICE,
a dual-run attention-head intervention, on Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B base.
A question-aware matcher audit reveals that apparent transfer can be a same-entity
wrap artifact rather than a transferred causal object. On Llama, the published
context-K10 transfer (+15 percentage points (pp), 95% CI [+8, +23]) is one such
wrap: the audited number is +1 [+0, +3] (null), with 17 of 17 original INT4
flips naming the same context entity. The locked Llama parametric pair (clean
K2) remains detected at n=100 on both precisions under gold-PAR-only counting
(−8 [−14, −3] on FP16; −7 [−12, −2] on INT4), though no matched randomhead control exists for this cell. On Gemma, identified context-suppression H −
remains detected versus a locked random-K control but is attenuated on INT4
(inclusive −19 [−27, −11] vs. −1 [−5, +2] on FP16; −11 [−18, −4] vs. −1
[−5, +3] on INT4); context-K10 enlarge is a small real effect (+7 [+2, +12] /
+5 [+1, +9]) but not INT4-specific, and the published raw peak L27 H6 is dead
as a wrap artifact. A same-sign ∆ is therefore not evidence of transfer without
question-aware matching, entity confirmation, and a matched random-head control.

I NTRODUCTION

When retrieved context contradicts a model’s parametric memory, a small set of attention heads can
be used, at test time, to steer whose word the model trusts (Li et al., 2025). This paper asks whether
that residual-stream geometry is an FP16 object or a property of the model that transfers under compression: can apparent FP16→INT4 transfer be trusted from output labels alone? Accuracy can fall
from forgetting, terse decoding, or a change in source arbitration; a transferred intervention distinguishes those cases. Our test identifies causal heads on FP16, verifies them by dual-run steering,
then applies the locked heads to GPTQ INT4 on Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B base (Grattafiori
et al., 2024; Rivière et al., 2024).
The contribution is a causal, head-level transfer test under this GPTQ INT4 recipe (Frantar et al.,
2023), paired with a question-aware matcher audit that re-evaluates the output labels. We demonstrate that behavioral efficacy is not, by itself, evidence that the same causal object transfers under
compression: without a question-aware matcher, apparent transfer can be a same-entity wrap artifact
rather than a true circuit.
Can apparent FP16→INT4 transfer be trusted? On Llama, the FP16-identified context K10 effect
(+8 [+2, +14]) transfers onto INT4 as a large same-sign output (+15 [+8, +23]) under the original
matcher, but the question-aware audit reveals this to be a same-entity wrap artifact. The audited
INT4 effect is a null +1 [+0, +3], with 17 of 17 original flips naming the same context entity in
a role phrase. Native INT4 K10 leaves only a thin leftover, and reverse transfer is mostly a scorer
artifact, showing that apparent behavioral transfer can mask a dead causal object.
Does a locked intervention still elicit the intended entity? On Llama, the locked parametric pair
(clean K2) remains detected at n=100 under conservative gold-PAR-only counting on both precisions (−8 [−14, −3] on FP16; −7 [−12, −2] on INT4). While this establishes that a parametric
effect is detected after the entity audit, the absence of a matched random-head control leaves open
the possibility that a generic intervention of the same size could produce the same effect.

R ELATED W ORK

When retrieved context contradicts parametric memory, a language model must decide whose word
to trust (Xie et al., 2024). JuICE (Li et al., 2025) is a test-time, training-free dual-run intervention
on a small set of attention heads. The original JuICE models are base checkpoints, not Instruct,
a distinction developed in Methods (§3.2). We do not revive a memory-head versus context-head
dichotomy (Jin et al., 2024); the transfer test is whether a locked intervention remains the same
causal object after GPTQ. Prior compression work measures recall without a conflicting passage
(Wang et al., 2026) or RAG accuracy without constructed conflicts (Yazan et al., 2024). Neither
tests whether an FP16-identified arbitration intervention still acts on the same object after INT4.
A drop in closed-book recall, or a stable RAG score, can occur without that intervention remaining
detected. We double-filter on facts that remain answerable at both precisions and then transfer locked
JuICE.

3.2

I NSTRUCT VERSUS BASE MECHANISTIC TARGET

JuICE was developed on base models (Li et al., 2025). We use dir par = par pos − ctx pos
as the directional parametric score. On Instruct with a chat template, parametric ID and steering fail
(Table 2): this score is suppressed by ∼1130×. This is a model-plus-prompt confound, so all INT4
transfer cells use base-identified heads.
FP16 base must steer before transfer. Table 6 reports question-aware context K10 at +4 [+0, +8]
(null) and clean K2 at gold-PAR-only −8 [−14, −3] (inclusive −15 [−22, −7]); those locked heads
are then applied to INT4 without re-identification.
3.3

GPTQ RECIPE AND EVALUATION

Stage 2 GPTQ uses gptqmodel 2.2, transformers 4.51.3, 4-bit quantization, group size
128, sym=True, and desc act=True. C4 is truncated to 512 tokens, with headline
NCALIB =128; Llama NCALIB =64 is a robustness check only. Gemma uses the same recipe with
GetHeadsGemma2Attention (GQA 16/8, head dim=256, logit softcap 50), eager attention, gptqmodel BACKEND.TORCH, and greedy T =0. Data is ConflictQA popular (Xie et al.,
2024). Stage 1 double-filters n=250 to n=164 items correct in closed-book mode at both precisions. Stage 2 uses the seed-42 n=100 IDs for Llama headline and diagnostic cells and for Gemma
headlines; Gemma n=50 is a robustness check. Reverse-transfer cells remain on the original n=50
IDs. Head identification uses |D|=4 shared calibration IDs, three-type JuICE completions, and
FAST POS ALPHAS=(1, 3, 5) and FAST NEG ALPHAS=(−1, −2).

4.2

C AN APPARENT FP16→INT4 TRANSFER BE TRUSTED ?

On Llama-3.1-8B base, apparent forward transfer of context K10 is a wrap artifact. Under the
original matcher, FP16 context K10 was +8 [+2, +14] and its INT4 NCALIB =128 transfer was
+15 [+8, +23]. After the question-aware audit, both cells are null: +4 [+0, +8] on FP16 and +1
[+0, +3] on INT4 (Table 7, Figure 2A). A random-K10 control confirms the null (+0 [−4, +4] on
FP16; −1 [−4, +2] on INT4). Entity audit of the 17 original INT4 transfer flips finds 17/17 sameentity wraps: the steered completion names the context entity in a role frame that the original scorer
mislabeled. Native INT4 K10 applied in-precision remains a thin leftover (+4 [+1, +8]); reverse
transfer of those heads onto FP16 is mostly a scorer artifact (see Appendix A for details on both).
Behavioral transfer alone is therefore not sufficient evidence of causal-object transfer when a scorer
can be satisfied by role-wrapped outputs.

4.4

I S TRANSFER UNIFORM ACROSS MODELS AND DIRECTIONS ?

On Gemma-2-9B base, identified context-suppression H − is detected but attenuated on INT4. At
n=100, inclusive ∆ is −19 [−27, −11] on FP16 versus random −1 [−5, +2], and −11 [−18, −4]
on INT4 versus random −1 [−5, +3] (Figure 4A). Specificity excludes 0 at both precisions (−18
[−27, −10] and −10 [−18, −2]), but the DiD is +8 [+1, +16], confirming FP16 specificity is larger.
Context K10 enlarge is a small real effect (FP16 +7 [+2, +12]; INT4 +5 [+1, +9]) but is not INT4specific, as the INT4 confidence interval overlaps with the +3 [+0, +7] random control (Figure 4B).
Gemma context K10 enlarge was run on INT4 at n=100, and on FP16 at both n=100 and n=50; the
INT4 transfer of this condition at n=50 was not run. The published raw parametric peak, L27 H6,
is dead as a wrap artifact: question-aware ∆ is a hard zero at n=50 on both precisions, and +0
[−3, +3] at n=100 INT4 (Figure 4C). The original L27 H6 at n=50 was compared against an INT4
random K1 control, but the corresponding FP16 random K1 control was not run at n=50. Finally,
Gemma clean-K2 enlarge (L31 H7, L39 H6) was run on FP16 at n=100 (−6 [−12, −1]); the INT4
transfer of that locked pair was not run, so we do not treat the missing cell as evidence that Gemma’s
parametric direction fails to transfer.
In both families, directional re-ranking exhibits a blind spot.
That score (par pos − ctx pos) promotes heads that strongly suppress context tokens over
heads that actually boost parametric tokens (Figure 3, Table 9). On Llama, L1 H4 is the raw
parametric peak (par pos= + 0.0577) but ranks only #29 under directional ranking. Both
Llama’s clean parametric pair (L20 H14, L26 H15) and Gemma’s (L31 H7, L39 H6) consist of
context suppressors, while L27 H6 is Gemma’s raw positive peak.

This evaluation distinguishes between a behavioral effect on the scorer, an audited causal effect, and
structural circuit preservation. Our evidence supports the presence of audited causal effects for the
surviving interventions, not structural circuit preservation. The two families reject a category-level
account of transfer: the evidence supports neither “context survives compression” nor “parametric
memory breaks.” Rather, whether a particular locked intervention remains detected after GPTQ is
model- and direction-dependent.

The cross-family regularity is methodological: a same-sign ∆ is not evidence of transfer without

C ONCLUSION

If a source-arbitration intervention is computed in FP16 residual-stream geometry, GPTQ INT4
does not apply a uniform tax to that object. However, these findings evaluate this specific GPTQ
INT4 recipe (4-bit, group size 128), not quantization in general. Under this recipe, Llama clean K2
is detected at n=100 under gold-PAR-only counting. Gemma identified H − versus random K is
detected but attenuated on INT4. Gemma context-K10 enlarge is a small real effect, but its INT4
confidence interval overlaps with the random control. Both Llama context K10 and Gemma L27 H6
fail to transfer and are dead as wrap artifacts. Which intervention remains detected is therefore
model-, direction-, and matcher-specific, not determined by source category.
