                                                           A BSTRACT
025
026
               Post-training quantization can preserve answer accuracy while changing the com-
027
               putation that selects between retrieved context and parametric memory. We test
028
               this possibility with JuICE, a dual-run attention-head intervention, on Llama-
029            3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B base models under GPTQ INT4. We identify source-
030            arbitration heads on FP16, lock them, and test forward transfer, random-head con-
031            trols, and in-precision application on 50 held-out conflict items. On Llama, an
032            FP16 context-head set transfers head-specifically to INT4 (+18 percentage points
033            (pp), 95% CI [+8, +30]); the FP16 parametric intervention is not detected after
034            quantization despite multiple recovery attempts. On Gemma, the directional rank-
035            ing misses the raw positive parametric peak: steering that single head transfers
036            head-specifically from −12 pp [−22, −4] on FP16 to −8 pp [−16, −2] on INT4,
037
               whereas a random-head control is exactly zero. The same ranking blind spot ap-
               pears in both families. A Gemma context-suppression effect is detected at both
038
               precisions but is not distinguishable from its locked random-K control, illustrating
039
               why intervention efficacy alone is insufficient evidence of circuit transfer. Thus,
040            quantization transfer is neither uniformly preserved nor determined by source cat-
041            egory; circuit-level claims require direct controls of intervention specificity.
042

044   1   I NTRODUCTION
045
046
      When retrieved context contradicts a model’s parametric memory, a small set of attention heads
047
      can be used, at test time, to steer whose word the model trusts (Li et al., 2025). This paper asks
048   whether that residual-stream geometry is an FP16 object or a property of the model that survives
049   compression: if JuICE is computed on FP16, does it still steer after GPTQ INT4, even on items
050   whose facts survive quantization? Accuracy can fall from forgetting, terse decoding, or a change in
051   source arbitration; a transferred intervention distinguishes those cases. Figure 1 is the test: identify
052   causal heads on FP16, verify them by dual-run steering, then apply the locked heads to GPTQ
053   INT4, on Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B base (Grattafiori et al., 2024; Rivière et al., 2024), after a
      behavioral check on Llama Instruct.

054
      The contribution is a causal, head-level transfer test under GPTQ INT4 (Frantar et al., 2023), not
055   a new accuracy table. Two objects transfer head-specifically—Llama context K10 and Gemma’s
056   raw parametric peak—but neither source category predicts transfer, and a detected Gemma context-
057   suppression effect is not distinguishable from a locked random-head control. Behavioral efficacy is
058   not, by itself, evidence that the same causal object survived compression.
059
      Concretely, we show:
060
061         1. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, GPTQ INT4 preserves closed-book recall on a double-filtered
062            set while shifting conflict arbitration toward context (MR⋆ −23.8 pp [−31.7, −17.1];
063            cluster-robust AME +10.3 pp [+3.1, +17.5]).
064         2. On Llama-3.1-8B base, FP16 context K10 transfers head-specifically to INT4 (+18
065            [+8, +30] vs. random-K10 −6 [−14, 0]) and is recovered by native INT4 identification.
066            Reverse transfer is a scorer artifact, not the same circuit. Parametric JuICE is a detected
067            FP16 effect and a null at α=1 on INT4 after rescale, raw-peak, native re-ID, and suppres-
068            sion repairs.
069         3. On Gemma-2-9B base, directional ranking again buries the raw parametric peak. Steering
070            that peak (L27 H6) transfers head-specifically (−12 [−22, −4] on FP16; −8 [−16, −2] on
071            INT4; random-K=1 exactly 0). Context K10 enlarge is a ceiling null; context suppression
072            is detected at both precisions but its identified-versus-random contrast, and the difference-
073            in-differences, include 0.
074         4. The transferable object is therefore family-specific. Quantization-selectivity is not a uni-
075            versal “context lives / parametric dies” rule.

      2   R ELATED W ORK
078
079
      When retrieved context contradicts parametric memory, a language model must decide whose word
080
      to trust (Xie et al., 2024). JuICE (Li et al., 2025) is a test-time, training-free dual-run intervention
081   on a small set of attention heads. The original JuICE models are base checkpoints, not Instruct—a
082   distinction that is load-bearing below (§4.2). We do not revive a memory-head versus context-head
083   dichotomy (Jin et al., 2024); the transfer test is whether a locked intervention remains the same
084   causal object after GPTQ. Prior compression work measures recall without a conflicting passage
085   (Wang et al., 2026) or RAG accuracy without constructed conflicts (Yazan et al., 2024). Neither
086   tests whether an FP16-identified arbitration intervention still acts on the same object after INT4.
087   A drop in closed-book recall, or a stable RAG score, can occur without that circuit surviving. We
088   double-filter on facts that remain answerable at both precisions and then transfer locked JuICE.

      4.1    S TAGE 1: BEHAVIORAL ARBITRATION ON I NSTRUCT
181
182   Stage 1 asks, before any head is identified: when a fact survives INT4, does conflict arbitration still
183   look like FP16? We compare FP16 Instruct to a third-party GPTQ-INT4 Instruct checkpoint on 250
184   ConflictQA popular items, then restrict to the 164 items closed-book correct at both precisions.
185
      MR⋆ on the shared 164 is the primary estimand (Table 2): INT4 −23.8 pp [−31.7, −17.1], excluding
186
      0. Closed-book is nearly stable (−7 items); unfiltered MR also drops −17.6 pp, so this is not
187   forgetting that vanishes after the double filter. Cluster-robust logit AME on follows context
188   is +10.3 pp [+3.1, +17.5] (Table 3). Label counts (Appendix Tables 15–16) show a parametric
189   collapse and a rise in context. A first-pass substring scorer inflated AME (+20.6 pp) by counting
190   denials as context; numbers here use the corrected scorer. Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct was a null screen
191   and is not mixed into the 8B claim. Stage 1 and Stage 2 INT4 files remain different artifacts.

194

196   ID and steering fail (Table 4): directional par is suppressed ∼1100×. That is a model+prompt
197   confound. All INT4 transfer cells use base-identified heads.
198   FP16 base must steer before transfer. Table 5: context K10 +10 [+2, +20]; clean K2 (L20 H14,
199   L26 H15) −8 [−16, −2]. Those locked heads are applied to INT4 without re-ID.

417
