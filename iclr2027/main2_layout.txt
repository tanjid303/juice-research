029   1   I NTRODUCTION
030
031   When retrieved context contradicts a model’s parametric memory, a small set of attention heads can
032   be used, at test time, to steer whose word the model trusts (Li et al., 2025). This paper asks whether
033   that residual-stream geometry is an FP16 object or a property of the model that transfers under com-
034   pression: can apparent FP16→INT4 transfer be trusted from output labels alone? Accuracy can fall
035   from forgetting, terse decoding, or a change in source arbitration; a transferred intervention distin-
036   guishes those cases. Our test identifies causal heads on FP16, verifies them by dual-run steering,
037   then applies the locked heads to GPTQ INT4 on Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B base (Grattafiori
038
      et al., 2024; Rivière et al., 2024).
039   The contribution is a causal, head-level transfer test under this GPTQ INT4 recipe (Frantar et al.,
040   2023), paired with a question-aware matcher audit that re-evaluates the output labels. We demon-
041   strate that behavioral efficacy is not, by itself, evidence that the same causal object transfers under
042   compression: without a question-aware matcher, apparent transfer can be a same-entity wrap artifact
043   rather than a true circuit.
044   Can apparent FP16→INT4 transfer be trusted? On Llama, the FP16-identified context K10 effect
045   (+8 [+2, +14]) transfers onto INT4 as a large same-sign output (+15 [+8, +23]) under the original
046   matcher, but the question-aware audit reveals this to be a same-entity wrap artifact. The audited
047   INT4 effect is a null +1 [+0, +3], with 17 of 17 original flips naming the same context entity in
048   a role phrase. Native INT4 K10 leaves only a thin leftover, and reverse transfer is mostly a scorer
049   artifact, showing that apparent behavioral transfer can mask a dead causal object.
050   Does a locked intervention still elicit the intended entity? On Llama, the locked parametric pair
051   (clean K2) remains detected at n=100 under conservative gold-PAR-only counting on both preci-
052   sions (−8 [−14, −3] on FP16; −7 [−12, −2] on INT4). While this establishes that a parametric
053   effect is detected after the entity audit, the absence of a matched random-head control leaves open
      the possibility that a generic intervention of the same size could produce the same effect.

      2      R ELATED W ORK

098
099
      When retrieved context contradicts parametric memory, a language model must decide whose word
      to trust (Xie et al., 2024). JuICE (Li et al., 2025) is a test-time, training-free dual-run intervention
100
      on a small set of attention heads. The original JuICE models are base checkpoints, not Instruct,
101
      a distinction developed in Methods (§3.2). We do not revive a memory-head versus context-head
102   dichotomy (Jin et al., 2024); the transfer test is whether a locked intervention remains the same
103   causal object after GPTQ. Prior compression work measures recall without a conflicting passage
104   (Wang et al., 2026) or RAG accuracy without constructed conflicts (Yazan et al., 2024). Neither
105   tests whether an FP16-identified arbitration intervention still acts on the same object after INT4.
106   A drop in closed-book recall, or a stable RAG score, can occur without that intervention remaining
107   detected. We double-filter on facts that remain answerable at both precisions and then transfer locked
      JuICE.

255   original matcher, FP16 context K10 was +8 [+2, +14] and its INT4 NCALIB =128 transfer was

260
      transfer of those heads onto FP16 is mostly a scorer artifact (see Appendix A for details on both).

      5    D ISCUSSION
364
365
366   This evaluation distinguishes between a behavioral effect on the scorer, an audited causal effect, and
367   structural circuit preservation. Our evidence supports the presence of audited causal effects for the
368   surviving interventions, not structural circuit preservation. The two families reject a category-level
369   account of transfer: the evidence supports neither “context survives compression” nor “parametric
370   memory breaks.” Rather, whether a particular locked intervention remains detected after GPTQ is
371   model- and direction-dependent.
372   The cross-family regularity is methodological: a same-sign ∆ is not evidence of transfer without
373   a question-aware matcher, entity-level confirmation, and a matched random-head control. Llama
374   context K10 and Gemma L27 H6 both produced large same-sign outputs that an entity-level audit
375   revealed to be role-phrase wrap artifacts. Conversely, while Llama clean K2 remains detected after
376   the entity audit, the absence of a matched random-head control leaves open the possibility that a
377   generic intervention of the same size could produce the same effect. Directional ranking alone is not
      interchangeable with a source-specific circuit without these checks.

      6    C ONCLUSION
409
      If a source-arbitration intervention is computed in FP16 residual-stream geometry, GPTQ INT4
410
      does not apply a uniform tax to that object. However, these findings evaluate this specific GPTQ
411
      INT4 recipe (4-bit, group size 128), not quantization in general. Under this recipe, Llama clean K2
412   is detected at n=100 under gold-PAR-only counting. Gemma identified H − versus random K is
413   detected but attenuated on INT4. Gemma context-K10 enlarge is a small real effect, but its INT4
414   confidence interval overlaps with the random control. Both Llama context K10 and Gemma L27 H6
415   fail to transfer and are dead as wrap artifacts. Which intervention remains detected is therefore
416   model-, direction-, and matcher-specific, not determined by source category.
