                                                  A BSTRACT
008
009
               Post-training quantization can preserve answer accuracy while changing the com-
010
               putation that selects between retrieved context and parametric memory. We test
011
               this possibility with JuICE, a dual-run attention-head intervention, on Llama-
012            3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B base models under GPTQ INT4. We identify source-
013            arbitration heads on FP16, lock them, and test forward transfer, random-head con-
014            trols, and in-precision application on 50 held-out conflict items. On Llama, an
015            FP16 context-head set transfers head-specifically to INT4 (+18 percentage points
016            (pp), 95% CI [+8, +30]); the FP16 parametric intervention is not detected after
017            quantization despite multiple recovery attempts. On Gemma, the directional rank-
018            ing misses the raw positive parametric peak: steering that single head transfers
019            head-specifically from −12 pp [−22, −4] on FP16 to −8 pp [−16, −2] on INT4,
020
               whereas a random-head control is exactly zero. The same ranking blind spot ap-
               pears in both families. A Gemma context-suppression effect is detected at both
021
               precisions but is not distinguishable from its locked random-K control, illustrating
022
               why intervention efficacy alone is insufficient evidence of circuit transfer. Thus,
023            quantization transfer is neither uniformly preserved nor determined by source cat-
024            egory; circuit-level claims require direct controls of intervention specificity.

027   1   I NTRODUCTION
028
029   When retrieved context contradicts a model’s parametric memory, a small set of attention heads
030   can be used, at test time, to steer whose word the model trusts (Li et al., 2025). This paper asks
031   whether that residual-stream geometry is an FP16 object or a property of the model that survives
032   compression: if JuICE is computed on FP16, does it still steer after GPTQ INT4, even on items
033   whose facts survive quantization? Accuracy can fall from forgetting, terse decoding, or a change in
034   source arbitration; a transferred intervention distinguishes those cases. Figure 1 is the test: identify
035
      causal heads on FP16, verify them by dual-run steering, then apply the locked heads to GPTQ
      INT4, on Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B base (Grattafiori et al., 2024; Rivière et al., 2024), after a
036
      behavioral check on Llama Instruct.
037
038   The contribution is a causal, head-level transfer test under GPTQ INT4 (Frantar et al., 2023), not
039   a new accuracy table. Two objects transfer head-specifically—Llama context K10 and Gemma’s
040   raw parametric peak—but neither source category predicts transfer, and a detected Gemma context-
041
      suppression effect is not distinguishable from a locked random-head control. Behavioral efficacy is
      not, by itself, evidence that the same causal object survived compression.
042

080   2   R ELATED W ORK
081
082   When retrieved context contradicts parametric memory, a language model must decide whose word
083   to trust (Xie et al., 2024). JuICE (Li et al., 2025) is a test-time, training-free dual-run intervention
084   on a small set of attention heads. The original JuICE models are base checkpoints, not Instruct—a
085   distinction that is load-bearing below (§4.2). We do not revive a memory-head versus context-head
086   dichotomy (Jin et al., 2024); the transfer test is whether a locked intervention remains the same
087   causal object after GPTQ. Prior compression work measures recall without a conflicting passage
088
      (Wang et al., 2026) or RAG accuracy without constructed conflicts (Yazan et al., 2024). Neither
      tests whether an FP16-identified arbitration intervention still acts on the same object after INT4.
089
      A drop in closed-book recall, or a stable RAG score, can occur without that circuit surviving. We
090
      double-filter on facts that remain answerable at both precisions and then transfer locked JuICE.

196   4.1    S TAGE 1: BEHAVIORAL ARBITRATION ON I NSTRUCT
197
198   Stage 1 asks, before any head is identified: when a fact survives INT4, does conflict arbitration still
199   look like FP16? We compare FP16 Instruct to a third-party GPTQ-INT4 Instruct checkpoint on 250
200   ConflictQA popular items, then restrict to the 164 items closed-book correct at both precisions.
201   MR⋆ on the shared 164 is the primary estimand (Table 2): INT4 −23.8 pp [−31.7, −17.1], excluding
202   0. Closed-book is nearly stable (−7 items); unfiltered MR also drops −17.6 pp, so this is not
203   forgetting that vanishes after the double filter. Cluster-robust logit AME on follows context
204   is +10.3 pp [+3.1, +17.5] (Table 3). Label counts (Appendix Tables 15–16) show a parametric
205   collapse and a rise in context. A first-pass substring scorer inflated AME (+20.6 pp) by counting
206   denials as context; numbers here use the corrected scorer. Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct was a null screen
